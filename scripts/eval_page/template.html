Projection scoreboard

The theories of presupposition projection that linglib formalizes, scored against recorded experiments. Every mark is the right-hand side of a theorem, reduced by Lean when this page was built; follow a mark to its theorem.

✓right: the prediction matches the observation ✗wrong: it does not –silent: the theory predicts nothing here, or nothing was observed

Verdicts

Rows are theories, columns the observations. On Chemla (2009) each column is a different way for a theory to parse the same sentences, with the number of sentences whose parse it stipulates.

The observations

As printed in the papers and recorded in Linglib/Data/Experiments.

How a mark is computed

Each step is a named declaration, so a disagreement with a verdict can be traced to the step it comes from.

  1. Fact

    A judgment as printed, with its table and page: Data.Experiments.

  2. Shared parse

    The sentence in the fragment every theory reads: Presupposition.Formula, Presupposition.QuantifiedClause.

  3. Theory's parse

    By a rule on the shared parse, or stipulated sentence by sentence, at a counted cost: Eval.Parser.

  4. Acceptance

    Which contexts accept the parse; a theory without a condition for a sentence is silent on it: Eval.Projection.Theory.

  5. Linking hypothesis

    A stated rule from acceptance to the judgment subjects give: Theory.Predicts, Chemla2009a.PredictsUniversal.

  6. Verdict

    The prediction against the observation: Eval.Verdict.
